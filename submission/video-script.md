## Intro options — the opening ~30s (pick one)

**Option A — cold-open hook, then a one-line intro (recommended).** This is what the timed script below uses. Open on the bill so the confusion is *felt*, then introduce the team after the hook has landed.
`[ON SCREEN: the bill, slowly zoom to the $7,811 total and the wall of codes]`

> "This is a hospital bill for 343 — and to most people, it might as well be in another language. Dozens of codes, no explanations, and a due date that won't wait. Meet **Rosa**. Her father just came home from the ER, and she's holding this with no idea what's real, what's a mistake, or what to even ask. And she's not alone — about **100 million Americans carry medical debt**. Hi, we're **[team name]**, and we built **MediAudit AI**: a tool that turns a scary medical bill into plain English, a checklist, and the exact words to say. Let me show you."

**Option B — name first, then hook fast.** Use only if your team wants names up front; it's slightly weaker because it delays the emotional hook.

> "Hi, we're **[team name]**, High School Track, and this is **MediAudit AI**. Here's the problem we're solving. `[bill on screen]` This is a hospital bill for 343 dollars. To Rosa — a caregiver whose dad just left the ER — it's a wall of codes she can't read, can't trust, and doesn't know what to do with. Around 100 million Americans face this. We turn that confusion into clarity."

**Delivery tips for the intro**
- Slow down on the very first line — let "$7,811… another language" land. That's the hook.
- Show the bill on screen as you speak, so the confusion is *felt*.
- Name a real person (Rosa) — never "people" or "everyone" (the brief marks vague users down).
- Warm, calm tone — you're speaking for someone under stress, not selling software.
- Keep it ≤ 30 seconds; if it runs long, cut adjectives, not the user.

## 0:00 – 0:30 · HOOK (problem + specific user + 1-line intro)
`[ON SCREEN: the bill, slowly zoom to the $7,811 total and the wall of codes]`

"This is a hospital bill for 7,811 dollars — and to most people, it might as well be in another language. Dozens of codes, no explanations, and a due date that won't wait.

Meet **Rosa**. Her father just came home from the ER, and she's holding this with no idea what's real, what's a mistake, or what to even ask. And she's not alone — about **100 million Americans carry medical debt**.

Hi, we're **[team name]**, and we built **MediAudit AI**: a tool that turns a scary medical bill into plain English, a checklist, and the exact words to say. Let me show you."

## 0:30 – 1:00 · THE SOLUTION + WHY AI
`[ON SCREEN: the MediAudit landing page — the "Crisis-to-Action Translator" hero]`

"Rosa snaps a photo of her bill, and in seconds she gets it back in **plain English**: what each charge means, which ones to question, and the exact words to say on the phone.

Why does this need AI instead of a Google search? Because a search can't read *Rosa's* bill, decode *her* specific codes, or write next steps for *her* charges and *her* state. MediAudit reads the actual document and personalizes everything."


## 1:25 – 3:05 · WALKTHROUGH + HOW THE AI WORKS
`[ON SCREEN: click "Try a sample bill"; let the loading stages play]`

"Here's how it works. First, **vision** reads every line and **masks any personal info** — names, ID numbers — so it's never sent off.

Then the real work: each charge is **cross-referenced** against a built-in knowledge base of typical prices and billing rules. The AI reasons through it — and then a **second, independent AI pass re-checks every flag**, confirming, adjusting, or disputing it.

`[ON SCREEN: the dossier appears — scroll slowly through each part]`

And here's the result. Up top: what was charged, what looks off, and the estimated overcharge.

**Part one** translates every code into plain English.

**Part two** shows the flagged charges — and notice each one **cites the exact line from the bill**, shows a **fair-price range**, and a **'verified' badge** from that second AI pass.

**Part three** is a ready-to-read **phone script**. **Part four**, a **step-by-step checklist**.

It even estimates whether Rosa qualifies for the hospital's **financial assistance**, and links her to **real help, like 211**.

`[ON SCREEN: toggle Español, then Simple language; then click Save / Print]`

And with one click, the whole thing switches to **Spanish**, or to **plain, simpler language** — and she can **print it** and walk it into the billing office."

## 1:00 – 1:25 · WHO BENEFITS
`[ON SCREEN: the "Why this matters" stats, then the Español toggle / the 211 resources]`

"Rosa is one story — but tens of millions face the same wall. **Uninsured and low-income families** hit hardest by surprise bills. **Older adults** on fixed incomes. People who speak **more Spanish than English** when they're trying to advocate for themselves. **Young adults** getting their first medical bill with no one to ask.

MediAudit is built for exactly these people — which is why it works in **plain language**, in **Spanish**, and points to **real local help** like 211 and hospital charity care."


## 3:05 – 3:45 · RESPONSIBLE AI (risk + mitigation + human-in-the-loop)
`[ON SCREEN: the "AI estimate" disclaimer, a "disputed on review" badge, the "Responsible by design" cards]`

"This is about money and health, so being responsible matters. Our biggest **risk** is that a stressed person treats an AI guess as fact.

So we built three **safeguards**. One: every number is clearly **labeled an estimate**, not a lookup. Two: that **independent second pass** catches and disputes weak flags before Rosa ever sees them. And three — the most important — MediAudit **never decides whether to dispute or pay, and never files anything**.

It hands Rosa the understanding and the words; **she** verifies against her records and makes the call. The **human stays in control**."

## 3:45 – 4:15 · CLOSE (impact)
`[ON SCREEN: back to the finished dossier / the brand]`

"That's MediAudit AI — turning a moment of confusion into **clarity and a clear next step**, for the people who need it most.

It's built with Google's **Gemini 3 Flash** and Next.js, and every tool is disclosed in our submission. Thanks for watching."
